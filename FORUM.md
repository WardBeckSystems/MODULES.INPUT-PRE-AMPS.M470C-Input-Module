## Ward Beck pre's

_WBS INFO · May 26, 2007|40_

> Ward Beck had a design that worked and put the same thing in an entire series so the pre-amp is almost identical in all of the L series it's how the signal goes out of the module that is different. It really depends on here you tap the output. 
>
> The M470's are the most flexible in terms of routing, so if you're looking for a console with flexibility fit them with M470's. within the M470's their are several variations different aux buses, pan pots master outputs. The big one and most flexible is the M470C it has 4 Aux buses, 6 sub group sends and pan pot. since some consoles were used as mono only, some M470's dont even have a pan pot. All M470's are swappable, so if you have a console with them any variation will pop in without any problems. I keep a set of M470C's kicking around so when a M470A module in my console dies or needs service they are standing by. 
>
> The M490's are identical pre-amps as th M470's just smaller with a fader fitted in to the module. they were used in radio broadcast consoles. Limited routing and they have a very flexible remote button section. 
> The M480 is a great module it combines a bit of the L series pre-amp and the EQ of say a scaled back M472 (the M480 has fixed LF and HF points) the M480's were only fitted in the T1202 transportable mixer. 
>
> The M460's use a different transformer and a different booster amp setup various eq and routing options are availible on these modules, they make great rack mounted pieces
> The M461 is identical input section as M460's but has no EQ
> The M441's are the most sought after and the closest to the original neve design that was copied. Best transformers, detended pots very little circuit boards all hand made. great pieces! 
> The M600 is the same vintage as the M441's they were designed to be remote pre-amps or used in intercom racks. they share some components as the M441's but are a rack tray module with only a gain pot not a console module
>
> i hope this helps, and if I'm wrong on some details sorry in advance

## My new monitor section  I need opinions

_MY WBS CONSOLE · Jun 3, 2007|40_

> So this is my new monitoring/master section. It's not built yet still in the planning state so that's why i wanted post it and ask for any opinions or suggestions. I've gutted everything and I'm starting from scratch. The plan is to use (14) 32 channel mogami snakes to connect the console to the patch field. Yes its an insane project and i expect it to take 2 years to finish :-) 
>
> top left: master modules. I wanted to put knobs instead of faders but i thought this was cooler. M477 for M1 fader for M1 and M2 then M477 for M2 then a space and an M477 for the Comb fader. The reason for this is the M477 are the hottest running module. they take the biggest beating. I've have them so hot that they were burning the PCB! I plan to cut the faders down to fit the module space
>
> Middle Left: the console is in two sections 1-32 and 33-64. the left side will feed aux 1-4 and the right will be 5-8 so (4) M479 aux modules. (2)M457 mixer modules all inputs and outputs are on the patchbay then a speaker selector for the main monitors
>
> Bottom left: (6)M473A sub master modules. Since the M470C are a 6 bus console i need this it also allows for 5.1 surround mixing should i be crazy then the M451 talk back module that controls 1: headphone one and two 2: headphone three and four 3: Studio speaker 4: lounge paging system
>
> center top: (10) M498A selectors that feed the monitor modules they will have all the master busses, auxes and subgroups
>
> center middle: (8) M475 Monitor modules left and right control room speakers, Headphone mix 1-4, studio speaker, then lounge Page. all the pots from the M475 outputs will be cut out and all he volume will be controlled but the faders below that section. I'm always constantly adjusting monitor outs so this allowed a really hands on process.

## Ward Beck Project 800278

_MY WBS CONSOLE · Apr 5, 2008|41_

> http://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/e1/WBS_Project_800278.JPG
>
> I had installed a patch feild for my school (they have their own Ward Beck for their television studio), and in trade for my services I got this beauty. This picture is a year old; the console is now just a frame where the modules are sitting it a relatively air-tight environment to prevent any further oxidization of the contacts (they are in pretty bad shape right now).
>
> History: console was designed for CFTO's Mobile 1 (which I assume to be one of their earlier SAT trucks) in April 1980, where some revisions were made in the manual at least as there is dates from 1980 & 1987 on the console design blueprints. Somwhere around when Baton owned CFPL (1994-1995), this console came to London (most likely in the truck it was designed for) and has probably been sitting in a barn since CFPL dismantled their SAT trucks. In Fall 2006 all of the modules and manuals made its way to Saunders Secondary School, where I have had the pleasure of learning and experimenting with the console for my final years of high school. Now it sits empty and lonely, but at least with some insurance for a better fate than what was previously planned for this console; Saunders was about to chuck it, I would not let that happen.
>
> Specs: 24 - M470C
>  Mixture of M462/M462B
>  Six submasters
>  4 Auxilaries
>  2 - 4x2 M457 Premixers
>  4 - M475(x) Monitor Modules
>  2 - M498A 10x1 Selectors
>
> I wish I had a better home for this console, as it deserves better. Immediate plans for the console is an attached rack for a patch field, but I still have studies to attend to so nothing is going to happen to quickly with this console.
>
> I may have more pictures, as this one is of poor quality. I will definately make a thread for the school's console (L3242) in the immediate future.
